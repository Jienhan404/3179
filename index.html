Student satisfaction in Australia · 2025

Worth the flight?

Thousands of Malaysians pack their bags for an Australian university every year. Here is what the students already there say about the teaching, the support, the campus and each other.

9,515 Malaysians studying at Australian universities and colleges in 2025
77% of undergraduates rate their overall experience positively
83% are happy with learning resources, the highest-rated area
61% feel engaged with other students, the lowest-rated area

How to read the numbers. Every year Australia asks its university students to rate their experience. A score of 80% means 80 in every 100 students gave a positive answer. Unless stated, figures are for undergraduate (bachelor's degree) students in 2025.

1 · The journey

Fewer Malaysians go than before, and nearly half land in Victoria

Malaysian numbers peaked at about 18,400 in 2010. They slid through the 2010s, dropped sharply when borders closed during COVID-19, and have settled at roughly half the peak.

Malaysia is still the tenth-largest source of international students, although China and India send far more. Of every 100 students from the ten biggest home countries, only 2 are Malaysian.

Where they go

Melbourne's state, Victoria, hosts 4,337 Malaysians. That is more than New South Wales, Western Australia and Queensland combined.

Perth and Brisbane each draw about 1,150, while the Northern Territory has just 11.

Thicker lines and bigger circles mean more students. Lines run from Kuala Lumpur to each state capital for illustration; they are not flight paths.

2 · The big picture

Satisfaction has recovered from COVID-19, but two areas lag behind

The survey groups its questions into five areas of university life, plus one question on the overall experience.

Strengths: learning resources, skills development and teaching quality all sit above 80%.

Challenges: student support is stuck near 71%, and only 61% of students feel engaged with their classmates. That score fell to 45% when classes moved online in 2020 and has been climbing back since.

3 · Where

The state matters less than the university

State averages for overall experience sit within about five points of each other. Individual universities are spread much more widely, from 67% to 96%. Use the menus to switch the maps to another area of university life.

4 · Compare universities

Universities are alike on resources and far apart on student engagement

Each dot is one of 43 universities. A tight cluster means universities are much the same; a long spread means your choice of university makes a real difference. Pick a university to see where it sits.

5 · Compare fields of study

What you study shapes your experience: computing and engineering students are the least satisfied

Fields are ordered from the happiest students to the least happy. Darker cells are higher scores. Agriculture and veterinary science lead on overall experience, while computing trails by more than 20 points. In every field, fewer than six in ten students feel they belong.

6 · Strengths and challenges

Great buildings and libraries, but few friendships beyond the classroom

These are the 37 individual questions behind the five areas, ranked from best to worst. Facilities fill the top of the list. The bottom is about people: getting useful feedback from teachers, finding financial advice, and mixing with other students outside class.

7 · What students value most

Support and teaching decide whether students are happy overall

If an area truly matters to students, universities that do well on it should also have happier students overall. Each panel tests that for one area. A steep line with dots hugging it means a strong link. “Link strength” runs from 0% (no link at all) to 100% (the area perfectly predicts overall happiness).

The weakest area nationally, engaging with other students, has almost no link with overall satisfaction. Students are forgiving of a quiet social life, but not of poor teaching or missing support.

8 · Who is happier?

International students rate support and classmates higher than locals do

Each row shows the gap between groups of students; the longer the bar between dots, the bigger the gap. Try “First year vs later years” to see satisfaction fade after the first year.

9 · When it goes wrong

Stress, mental health and money are the main reasons students think about quitting

Students who had seriously considered leaving their course were asked why. They could choose more than one reason.

Personal pressures top the list, well ahead of anything about the course itself. The good news: every one of the eight biggest reasons has eased since 2023. Numbers are the % of students giving each reason; a line sloping down means fewer students gave it.

10 · The Malaysian view

Malaysians come for safety and reputation, and most enjoy living there

Why they chose Australia

Safety, living costs and the reputation of Australian education matter to almost every Malaysian student.

Having friends or family already in Australia matters to fewer than half, far less than for other international students.

How they find life there

93% of Malaysian students rate living in Australia positively.

They are a little less happy than other nationalities with safety off campus and with finding work experience.

Countries are ordered by overall living experience. Malaysia is outlined in orange.

In short

Three things to remember before you apply

1

Look past the city

States score almost the same. The gap between individual universities, and between fields of study, is far bigger.

2

Ask about teaching and support

These two areas go hand in hand with overall happiness. Shiny facilities are good almost everywhere, so they tell you little.

3

Plan your social life and your budget

Making friends outside class is the weakest part of student life, and stress and money are the top reasons students think of leaving.
